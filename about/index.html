I’m Haotian Alex Xue, a graduate student in Architecture and Urban Design at Columbia University GSAPP.

I see architecture not only as the production of buildings, but as a way of observing, questioning, and reshaping the relationships that make a place. Growing up in a rapidly changing city has made me attentive to the spaces between established boundaries: between infrastructure and everyday life, built form and ecology, collective memory and new possibilities. These in-between conditions often become the starting points of my work.

The projects collected here move across architecture, urban design, research, and experimental forms of representation. Whether working with postwar urban fragments, industrial structures, or contested waterfronts, I am interested in how design can uncover overlooked values and invite new forms of occupation, interaction, and care. I approach sites not as blank canvases, but as layered environments shaped by people, materials, histories, and ongoing processes.

Making is central to how I think. Drawings, models, narratives, games, and spatial interventions are not simply ways to communicate a proposal; they are tools for testing ideas, engaging different perspectives, and revealing possibilities that conventional design methods might miss. Through these practices, I seek to make complex urban conditions more tangible and accessible.

Rather than proposing fixed solutions, my work explores architecture as an open-ended framework for adaptation and shared life. I hope to create spaces that retain memory while welcoming change, connect ecological and social systems, and expand the possibilities of everyday public experience.
