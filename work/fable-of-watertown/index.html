Project overview

What remains when every town begins to look the same?

Fable of Watertown examines how rapid economic development and tourism reshape Chinese water towns. Focusing on Wuzhen and Xinchang, the research follows the shift from places shaped by cultural memory and everyday life toward environments defined by repeated commercial models and tourist consumption.

Edward Relph’s ideas of place and rootedness frame the inquiry: identity emerges through relationships between people and their surroundings. The project asks what is lost when those relationships are replaced by standardized storefronts and curated images of tradition.

01 / Two towns, distinct histories

Wuzhen’s water-based trade, opera traditions, and indigo dyeing formed a distinctive cultural rhythm. Xinchang’s salt production and canal-based daily life shaped a different local identity. The paired reliefs establish the architectural ground, while the projection artwork adds scenes of everyday life. In both images, Xinchang appears above and Wuzhen below.

02 / An installation in four stages

Relief paintings provide a physical ground for moving images. The projection sequence shifts from scenes of local life toward commercial signs and tourist imagery, making the transformation of place visible on the same architectural surface.

View all four stages together

03 / Between material and image

The reliefs evoke historical place, while projected images alter how that place is perceived. The contrast between the persistent material surface and its changing visual content brings cultural memory and commercialization into the same frame.

In the audio, the Chinese fable of “picking up sesame seeds while losing the watermelon” becomes a metaphor for pursuing short-term profit at the expense of deeper cultural identity.
