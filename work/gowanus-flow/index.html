Project overview

A watershed is shared, even when decisions are individual.

Gowanus Flow explores how weather, infrastructure, and collective decisions shape water quality and flood resilience. Players release water marbles from a Catskill Reservoir zone, guiding them across a model watershed toward New York Harbor.

The board rotates between rounds. Players inherit a new zone and its existing infrastructure, while stranded marbles carry the consequences of earlier turns forward. The game uses a simplified physical system to make interdependence, uncertainty, and shared responsibility tangible.

01 / Making water systems tangible

A layered circular terrain turns the watershed into a physical field of paths, obstructions, and accumulation. Removable residential, industrial, green, and water infrastructure pieces change the conditions through which the marbles travel.

Weather cards determine the quantity of water released. Infrastructure cards introduce choices about the built environment. Rotation challenges players to respond to conditions created by someone else, linking individual strategy with the health of the whole watershed.

02 / How the game works

The game supports two to six players. Each begins with a reservoir zone, draws three infrastructure cards, may trade with other players, and selects one card for the first round. Players place infrastructure, draw a weather card, and collect the indicated number of marbles.

  1. Release water

    Beginning with the player to the right of the harbor, turns proceed counterclockwise. Release your marbles together and observe where they travel or become stuck.

  2. Record the consequences

    Award clean water points for reaching the harbor. Record pollution points where marbles become stranded; these marbles remain on the board unless later dislodged.

  3. Rotate the watershed

    Discard used cards and turn the board counterclockwise. Each player inherits the zone and infrastructure to their left, keeping their own score.

  4. Adapt and repeat

    Draw a new weather card and infrastructure card, then repeat the sequence. The changing board makes each round an encounter with inherited conditions.

Scoring at a glance

EventPoints
Marble reaches the harbor+3 clean water
Marble stuck in a green region+3 pollution
Marble stuck in a grey region+5 pollution
Dislodge a stuck marble without getting stuck+5 clean water

Additional rules for multiple stuck marbles and replacement obstructions are shown in the full rulebook, page 4.

Two measures of success

Individual score
Clean Water Points − Pollution Points

Collective goal
No more than 50% of the group’s total marbles may be polluted.

Game length
2 players: 4 rounds · 3 players: 6 rounds
4–6 players: one round per player
