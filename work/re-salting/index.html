Re-Salting

A salt shed that brings municipal infrastructure, public space, and material education into a shared urban landscape.

Design approach

Salt as urban landscape.

The proposal exposes the spatial and aesthetic qualities of salt: natural dunes, crystalline textures, and shifting volumes. A three-layer system preserves storage and redistribution below, introduces a public terrace in the middle, and gives the roof a silhouette informed by geological formations.

Seasonal rhythms and day–night differences organize logistical flows and public activities. The industrial facility becomes a civic interface where essential operations and everyday public experience can coexist.

01 / Design development

Material experiments → three inhabited layers

Experiments with salt and water inform a layered proposal: “The Shed” maintains storage and redistribution, “The Mountain” introduces a tactile public terrace, and “The Mirage” translates the salt landscape into an undulating roof.

02 / Design development

Operations below, public space above

Ground, second-floor, and roof plans explain how a working salt facility can accommodate public activity. Seasonal and day–night differences help organize the relationship between logistics and visitors.

03 / Design development

A landscape in section

The sections and elevation show how storage volumes, terraces, and the roof work together. The industrial building becomes a public encounter with the material it contains.
