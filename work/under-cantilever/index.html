Under Cantilever

Design approach

A long life for materials and space.

Prestressed and post-tensioned concrete enable a large cantilever and flexible, column-free space for receiving, sorting, and redistributing reclaimed materials. Separate circulation accommodates trucks and processing at ground level, with offices above.

The design studies how a durable structure could adapt as sea levels and urban needs change. A sequence of possible futures—from reclamation center to marketplace to pier—extends reuse from individual materials to the life of an entire building.

Project in detail

01 / Design development

Reuse across different timescales

Material lifespan studies and the South Boston site analysis connect building reclamation with long-term environmental change. The project treats the building itself as a resource that can remain useful as its surroundings evolve.

02 / Design development

A durable frame with room to change

Structural studies examine prestressing, post-tensioning, precast production, model testing, and assembly. The cantilever creates flexible space for material handling below.

03 / Design development

Separating industrial and staff movement

Trucks enter from the south for loading and processing. Staff enter from the north and move to the upper office zone, keeping administrative access distinct from material flows.

04 / Design development

From structural system to inhabited space

The sectional perspective and chunk model examine the relationship between the cantilever, enclosure, and occupied spaces. Physical models test the spatial consequences of the structural proposal.

05 / Design development

Three proposed futures

The portfolio imagines a reclamation center today, a marketplace after 30 years, and a pier after 70 years. These are design scenarios for reuse as water levels and urban programs change.
